What the research says

Our assessment design. And our brains.

Initial over-reliance on genAI is associated with weaker neural connectivity, lower sense of ownership, and poorer memory recall.

(Kosmyna et al. 2025)

Scaffolded, intentional AI integration that is aligned with evidence-based pedagogy can benefit learners.

(Kestin et al. 2025)

When students receive guidance on how to use AI tools from instructors, they show greater learning gains than when using the tools independently.

(Gu & Yan 2025)

Part Two

The under-13 problem.

The tools everyone is talking about were not built for your students. That does not mean the answer is nothing.

Know this before a device goes out

Almost none of it is legal for a nine-year-old.

13+
The consumer assistants
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot's consumer version all set a minimum age of 13, and some want parent consent to 18. Not for student hands in your building. Fine for yours.
COPPA
Why the line exists
Federal law restricts collecting personal information from children under 13 without verified parent consent. That is a district-level agreement, not a teacher-level decision.
Yes
Teacher-mediated platforms
MagicSchool's student side, SchoolAI, and Khanmigo are built for school use with a teacher dashboard and a district agreement behind them.

The rule of thumb: if there's no teacher dashboard and no district agreement, it isn't a student tool. It's your tool.

What it looks like when it's done right

At this age, AI belongs on your screen.

Not this

  • Handing a class of third graders an open chat window.
  • Having students make accounts on a consumer tool.
  • Letting it read aloud so you don't have to.

This

  • Project it and think out loud: “Watch, I'm going to ask it, and then we'll check whether it's right.”
  • Use it to make the thing, then teach the thing yourself.
  • Let it be wrong on purpose in front of them. That lesson lasts.

Your students will have an open chat window in about four years. What they'll remember is whether an adult ever showed them how to doubt it.

Elizabethton City Schools policy 4.214, issued July 16, 2024

Your board already wrote the rulebook.

Your own work
Green light
Draft communications and notes, develop instructional and administrative content, analyze data. The policy also tells the Director to build AI training into professional development. That is why we are here.
Student-facing
Approved programs only
Today that list is MagicSchool, Canva for Education, and Class Companion (6-12 only, so not your grades). It is being added to the district website. Anything not on it gets approved before a student touches it.
Never paste in
PII, and more than PII
No personally identifiable information, financial information, intellectual property, or other confidential material. And AI output does not get folded into proprietary work.
Trust but verify
You own the output
Verify outputs against reliable sources first. Disclose your AI use anywhere staying quiet would mislead a supervisor or run against the teacher code of ethics.

And note the student use rule: before students touch an approved tool, you have to teach them how to use it responsibly. At Pre-K-5, that instruction is the whole assignment.

Five minutes with the person next to you

Script the think-aloud.

1

Pick one small thing you would ask AI on your screen, in front of your class. Keep it tiny.

2

Now write the exact sentence you say when the answer comes back wrong. That sentence is the lesson.

You are not teaching them the tool. You are teaching them to doubt it out loud.

Before you go all in

The non-negotiables.

  • Student privacy first. Never paste a student's name, grades, behavior notes, or IEP into a public tool. Strip identifying details, or stay inside MagicSchool.
  • The 13+ rule is not a suggestion. No student accounts on consumer tools. Teacher-mediated platforms only, and approved before use, never after.
  • You are the editor. It will confidently hand you a decodable passage full of words your students haven't been taught yet. Read every word before it goes out.
  • Tell parents plainly. “I use AI to help me prepare materials. I do not use it to grade your child or to replace instruction.” Say it before someone asks.

Overuse
Let it write everything and it starts writing you. Your voice is the first thing to go, and you won't notice it leaving.
Obvious use
The dashes. “Delve.” “It's not just X, it's Y.” Perfect symmetry, zero specifics. Parents can spot it now. So can the families reading your newsletter.
Sameness
Same template, same gradient, same sparkle icon, same three cheerful bullets. Scroll any district's feed this fall and tell me they're different.
Everyone has the same tools now, so the tools stop being the advantage. Sounding like an actual person is about to be a marketable skill.
