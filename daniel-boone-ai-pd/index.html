Before we talk about stopping it

Why do students cheat?

They…

  • don't see the personal or social relevance or value of course tasks ("busy work").
  • feel like making a mistake constitutes failure.
  • perceive coursework to be more "private." (Denisova-Schmidt et al. 2022)
  • feel unsupported as learners and as people.
  • feel that their contributions don't matter to the instructor or to their peers.
  • are provided with only one or two high-stakes opportunities to demonstrate their knowledge, in one format.
  • care more about extrinsic motivation to earn points than intrinsic motivation to learn.

The hard truth

AI detectors will let you down.

Why detection fails

  • False positives flag honest students, especially English learners.
  • A few word swaps defeat most detectors in seconds.
  • You can't prove it, so accusations turn into your-word-vs-theirs.

What to lean on instead

  • Design assignments AI can't easily do for them.
  • Grade the process, not just the polished final product.
  • Make expected AI use explicit, so honesty is the easy path.
Assignment design beats policing

Five moves that hold up.

01
Bring writing back in the room
In-class drafts, handwritten or on locked devices, for the work that matters most.
02
Grade the journey
Outlines, drafts, and revisions, not just the final. AI can't fake a week of thinking.
03
Make it personal and local
Tie prompts to class discussion, this town, their own experience. Generic AI can't.
04
Ask them to defend it
A two-minute oral follow-up reveals who actually understands the work.

05. Let them use AI, then make them show and critique what it gave them.

FRICTION
Say it out loud, every assignment

A stoplight your students understand.

Green
AI encouraged. Brainstorm, get feedback, study. Use it and tell me how.
Yellow
AI allowed for parts. Ideas yes, final words no. Cite what you used.
Red
No AI. This one measures what's in your head, on your own.

Label every major assignment. Ambiguity is where cheating lives. And for green and yellow, students use district-approved programs only.

Washington County policy 4.214

Your district already wrote the rulebook. It's on your side.

Your own work
Green light
The policy explicitly allows staff to use AI for drafting, communications, instructional content, and data analysis. This training exists because the policy calls for it.
Student-facing
Approved programs only
Students may only use AI programs on the district's approved list, posted on the district website. There's a process to request new ones through the Director of Schools.
Never paste in
PII and confidential info
No personally identifiable information, financial information, or confidential district material goes into an AI system. Ever.
Trust but verify
You own the output
Verify AI output against reliable sources before you use it, and disclose AI use anywhere staying quiet would mislead a supervisor or violate the code of ethics.
03
Part Three

What you do Monday.

Small, concrete, no-permission-needed first steps.

Start here

Your first week.

  • Pick one free tool and use it for your own prep this week.Lesson plan, rubric, or parent email. Just one.
  • Add a green/yellow/red label to your next assignment.Tell students the rule out loud.
  • Redesign one assignment so AI can't just hand over the answer.Make it personal, in-class, or process-graded.
  • Have one honest conversation with a class about AI.They'll trust you more for it.
Before you go all in

The non-negotiables.

  • Student privacy first. Never paste a student's name, grades, or IEP into a public tool. Strip identifying details.
  • Mind the age rules. Most consumer tools want users 13+. Use the teacher-facing platforms for anything student-facing.
  • You are the editor. AI drafts, you decide. Check facts. It sounds confident even when it's wrong.
  • Know policy 4.214. Student-facing tools come from the approved list. When in doubt, ask before you adopt.

Overuse
Let it write everything and it starts writing you. Your voice is the first thing to go, and you won't notice it leaving.
Obvious use
The dashes. “Delve.” “It's not just X, it's Y.” Perfect symmetry, zero specifics. Parents can spot it now. So can your students.
Sameness
Same template, same gradient, same sparkle icon, same three cheerful bullets. Scroll any district's feed this fall and tell me they're different.
Everyone has the same tools now, so the tools stop being the advantage. Sounding like an actual person is about to be a marketable skill.
